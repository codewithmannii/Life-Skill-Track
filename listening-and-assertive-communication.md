# Listening and Assertive Communication

## 1. What are some ways to practice active listening?

-   Stay focused on what the other person is saying instead of getting
    lost in your own thoughts.
-   Let the speaker finish before you respond.
-   Show interest through small responses such as "I see," "That makes
    sense," or "Tell me more."
-   Use positive body language, like making eye contact and nodding.
-   Take notes when the conversation includes important details.
-   Repeat the main idea in your own words to make sure you understood
    it correctly.

## 2. What are the main ideas of reflective listening in Fisher's model?

-   Give the speaker your full attention and avoid distractions.
-   Allow pauses instead of filling every silence with unnecessary
    words.
-   Spend more time listening than speaking.
-   Pay attention to the speaker's personal experiences and feelings,
    not just general ideas.
-   Try to understand the situation from the speaker's point of view.
-   Respond with understanding and without judging them.
-   Notice changes in the speaker's tone, facial expressions, and body
    language.
-   Keep your response connected to the specific points the speaker has
    shared.

## 3. What usually makes listening difficult for you?

-   **Distractions:** Phone notifications, background noise, or too many
    open tabs can break my focus.
-   **Planning my reply:** Sometimes I think about what I will say next
    instead of listening properly.
-   **Interrupting:** I may cut in or try to complete the other person's
    sentence.
-   **Making assumptions:** I sometimes form an opinion before hearing
    the full explanation.
-   **Lack of interest:** It can be difficult to stay focused when the
    topic does not interest me.
-   **Stress or tiredness:** Worry, hunger, or low energy can affect my
    attention.
-   **Too much information:** I may find it hard to remember everything
    when many details are shared at once.
-   **Language differences:** An unfamiliar accent or difficult words
    can make it harder to understand the message.
-   **Missing body language:** I may focus only on the words and
    overlook the speaker's expressions or tone.
-   **Strong emotions:** If something upsets me, I may become defensive
    instead of listening calmly.
-   **Wandering thoughts:** My mind can drift to other things while
    someone is speaking.

## 4. What can you do to become a better listener?

-   Keep my phone away and reduce other distractions.
-   Give the speaker my full attention.
-   Wait until the person finishes before responding.
-   Explain the main point back in my own words.
-   Ask questions whenever something is unclear.
-   Pay attention to facial expressions, gestures, and tone of voice.
-   Listen with an open mind instead of judging too quickly.
-   Make active listening a regular habit in everyday conversations.

## 5. In which situations do you use a passive communication style?

-   When speaking with seniors, teachers, or managers, I may avoid
    sharing a different opinion.
-   When I am worried about an argument or someone's reaction, I may
    agree even if I do not want to.
-   In group discussions, I may stay quiet because I feel that others
    know more than I do.
-   I may accept extra work or favours even when I want to say no.
-   Sometimes I avoid asking questions because I do not want others to
    think I lack knowledge.
-   When I am new to a team or workplace, I may hesitate to express my
    thoughts.
-   With friends or family, I may keep quiet just to avoid disturbing
    the peace.

## 6. In which situations do you use an aggressive communication style?

-   When I am stressed, tired, or dealing with too much work, I may lose
    my patience.
-   If I feel ignored or disrespected, I may raise my voice to make my
    point.
-   When someone repeats the same mistake, I may react more strongly
    than necessary.
-   During disagreements, I may interrupt, blame others, or use harsh
    words.
-   If I keep my frustration inside for too long, I may suddenly lose my
    temper.
-   When I feel criticised, I may become defensive and respond angrily.
-   Delays, traffic, or long queues can sometimes make me impatient.
-   In competitive situations, I may focus more on proving that I am
    right than understanding the other person.

## 7. In which situations do you use a passive-aggressive communication style?

-   When I am upset but do not want to discuss the issue directly, I may
    show my frustration indirectly.
-   If I feel unappreciated, I may use sarcasm instead of explaining
    what bothered me.
-   When I disagree with a senior, I may say yes but not show real
    interest in completing the task.
-   If a friend hurts me, I may stop talking or give very short replies.
-   During group conflicts, I may complain to other people instead of
    speaking directly to the person involved.
-   If I feel jealous or resentful, I may make indirect comments or
    taunts.
-   When I find it difficult to say no, I may agree and then delay the
    work.
-   Sometimes I expect people to understand that I am upset without
    telling them what is wrong.

## 8. How can you make your communication more assertive?

-   **Understand what I want to say:** Think about my point before
    starting the conversation.
-   **Use "I" statements:** Explain my feelings or needs without blaming
    the other person. For example, "I feel uncomfortable when my point
    is interrupted."
-   **Be clear and respectful:** Say what I mean in a simple and direct
    way.
-   **Learn to say no:** Refuse politely when I cannot take on
    something.
-   **Keep my tone calm:** Even when I disagree, I should avoid shouting
    or using harsh words.
-   **Respect the other person's view:** Assertive communication also
    means listening to others.
-   **Ask questions when needed:** It is okay to ask for help or
    clarification.
-   **Start with small situations:** Practice expressing my opinions and
    needs in everyday conversations.
